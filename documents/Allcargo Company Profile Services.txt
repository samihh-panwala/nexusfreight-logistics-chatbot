2. Company Overview
Allcargo Logistics is an Indian integrated logistics company headquartered in Mumbai. It began in 1994 as a
cargo-handling operator at Jawaharlal Nehru Port and has grown into one of the largest players in India's
international supply-chain and freight-forwarding sector, with a global network spanning roughly 180 countries
through its subsidiary ECU Worldwide.
The company positions itself as a customer-centric, technology-driven logistics provider, offering everything from
small less-than-container-load shipments to full container loads, warehousing, and last-mile express delivery. It
operates across multiple business lines rather than a single service, which is what lets it serve customers ranging
from small exporters to large industrial manufacturers.
This breadth matters in practice because a single customer's shipment can touch several of these business lines at
once. A mid-sized exporter, for instance, might use Allcargo's international supply chain business to consolidate an
LCL shipment bound for Europe, rely on a CFS facility at the port of origin for export documentation and bonded
storage, and separately use the express distribution network to move finished goods domestically to the port in the
first place. Understanding the company as a connected set of services, rather than isolated business units, is
important context for answering a customer's question accurately, since a query that sounds like it belongs to one
service line often has a natural connection to another.
The customer base spans a wide range of shipment sizes and industries as a direct result of this multi-line structure.
A small exporter shipping a handful of pallets of textiles typically uses LCL consolidation and customs brokerage
support without ever needing a dedicated warehouse relationship. A large automotive or chemicals manufacturer, by
contrast, is more likely to be a contract logistics customer first — using an Allcargo industrial park for ongoing
warehousing and distribution — and only occasionally an international freight customer when a specific shipment
needs to cross a border. A retailer running e-commerce fulfilment may use the express distribution network almost
exclusively for last-mile delivery, with little or no involvement in the international or CFS side of the business at all.

3. Company History & Milestones
Allcargo's story starts with founder Shashi Kiran Shetty, who began his career in logistics as a trainee before starting
his first venture, Trans India Freight Services. In 1993 he founded what would become Allcargo Logistics, initially
focused on customs house agency and freight forwarding at JNPT, Mumbai.
A pivotal early move was becoming the India agent for Antwerp-based ECU-Line in 1995, which introduced the
company to Less-than-Container-Load (LCL) consolidation — combining multiple smaller shipments into a single
container to cut costs. This became a core part of the business. Below is a condensed timeline of key milestones.

International Supply Chain (ISC)
•
Less-than-Container-Load (LCL) consolidation — Allcargo is one of the largest non-vessel operating common
carriers (NVOCC) in this segment globally.
•
Full Container Load (FCL) / Neutral FCL freight forwarding.
•
Air freight forwarding and export/import handling.
•
Customs brokerage and documentation support.
•
Special cargo handling: hazardous (DG) cargo, ODC (over-dimensional cargo), reefer/temperature-controlled
shipments, ISO tank services.
In practice, LCL and FCL represent two different ways of buying container space rather than two different kinds of
shipment. A customer with a small volume of cargo that would not fill a container on its own uses LCL, paying only for
the space their cargo occupies while Allcargo consolidates it with other customers' cargo bound for the same
destination. A customer with enough volume to fill a container outright books FCL instead, and pays for the whole
container regardless of how full it is, in exchange for not having to share space or wait for consolidation. Neutral FCL
forwarding extends this to customers who want Allcargo to manage the booking and documentation for a full
container without Allcargo acting as the NVOCC of record.
It is also worth being explicit about what a customs house agent and a freight forwarder actually do, since both terms
describe Allcargo's earliest role at JNPT and both remain relevant to how the company operates today. A customs
house agent is licensed to prepare and file the paperwork needed to clear cargo through customs on a shipper's
behalf — declarations, duty calculations, and the physical presentation of documents to customs authorities. A freight
forwarder arranges the actual movement of cargo between origin and destination, coordinating with carriers, ports,
and warehouses, but does not necessarily own any of the ships, aircraft, or trucks involved. Allcargo has performed
both roles since its earliest days at JNPT, and the CFS and ICD network described in Section 4 exists specifically to
support the customs house agency side of that work.
Container Freight Stations (CFS) & Inland Container Depots (ICD)
•
CFS facilities at JNPT (Mumbai), Chennai, Mundra, and Kolkata handling export/import cargo, bonded and open
warehousing.
•
A joint-venture ICD at Dadri (National Capital Region) with Container Corporation of India (CONCOR).
•
Container maintenance, repair, and first/last-mile transportation to and from ports.
A CFS functions as an off-dock extension of a port: containers are moved from the port to the CFS for customs
examination, consolidation, deconsolidation, and storage, which relieves congestion at the port itself and gives
exporters and importers more space and time to complete documentation. An ICD serves a similar function but is
located further inland, allowing customs clearance to happen closer to a customer's own facility rather than requiring
cargo to be moved all the way to a coastal port first. Allcargo's joint-venture ICD at Dadri, run with Container
Corporation of India, gives customers in the Delhi NCR region this inland clearance option.
Contract Logistics & Warehousing
•
Third-party logistics (3PL) — warehousing, distribution, and production logistics for manufacturers.
•
Logistics and industrial parks located in hubs including Hyderabad, Bengaluru, Ahmedabad, and the Delhi NCR.
•
Sector-specific solutions for chemicals, e-commerce, automotive, and retail supply chains.
•
Kitting, reusable packaging, and pre-production/ordering-and-replenishment services.

This business line is distinct from the CFS and ICD network in that it is not tied to a customs clearance point at all — a
manufacturer can use an Allcargo logistics park purely for ongoing warehousing and domestic distribution, with no
export or import shipment involved. Sector-specific handling matters here because chemicals, for instance, require
different storage, handling, and safety compliance than e-commerce parcels or automotive components, and
Allcargo's contract logistics offering is built around accommodating those differences within the same network of
parks.
Kitting and reusable packaging services are worth explaining further, since they are less immediately self-explanatory
than warehousing itself. Kitting refers to assembling individual components into a pre-packaged set — for example,
bundling several related automotive parts into a single kit before they are shipped to an assembly line — which
reduces handling steps for the manufacturer downstream. Reusable packaging programs, common in automotive and
industrial supply chains, involve durable containers or crates that are returned and reused across multiple shipment
cycles rather than discarded after a single use, reducing both packaging cost and waste over time.
Express Distribution
•
Domestic surface and air express delivery across India (via the former Gati network, now merged into Allcargo).
•
Cash-on-delivery (COD), door-to-door delivery, and last-mile solutions, including an electric-vehicle fleet for
first/last-mile delivery.
Express distribution is the company's most customer-facing, retail-oriented business line, in contrast to the more
business-to-business nature of ISC, CFS, and contract logistics. It is the business line most likely to generate direct
end-consumer questions about delivery timing, cash-on-delivery availability, and last-mile tracking, since it is the part
of Allcargo's operation an individual recipient is most likely to interact with directly.
The 2020 acquisition of a controlling stake in Gati Ltd. is what gave Allcargo this domestic express capability in the
first place, since the company's earlier growth had been concentrated in international freight forwarding and CFS
operations rather than parcel-level domestic delivery. The subsequent 2023 rebranding to AllcargoGati and the full
2025 merger into the parent company reflect a gradual integration of that acquired network into the core Allcargo
brand, and a customer who previously dealt with Gati directly should now expect the same service to be presented
under the Allcargo name.
Digital Platform
•
ECU360 — an online platform for booking, real-time shipment tracking, and supply-chain visibility, relaunched in
2017 as part of the company's digital-transformation push.
ECU360 is the single platform that ties the other service lines together from a customer's point of view: the same
booking reference created for an LCL or FCL shipment can be tracked through customs clearance at a CFS, and
onward through final delivery, without the customer needing to contact each operational team separately. Any chatbot
question about tracking a shipment, checking a booking status, or getting a real-time update should point the
customer toward ECU360 as the primary self-service tool.
5. Global Network & Corporate Structure
Allcargo's international reach runs primarily through ECU Worldwide (formerly ECU-Line), its wholly owned NVOCC
subsidiary, which connects thousands of trade lanes across roughly 180 countries. The company has grown its
international footprint through acquisitions — including US-based Econocaribe Consolidators, a stake in FCL Marine
Rotterdam, and a majority stake in Swedish logistics firm Nordicon.

This structure means a customer's shipment is very often handled by more than one legal entity within the Allcargo
group without the customer needing to be aware of the distinction — an LCL booking might be issued under ECU
Worldwide as the NVOCC of record, while the domestic pickup and delivery legs are handled by the former Gati
network now fully merged into the parent company. For chatbot purposes, the group should generally be presented to
a customer as a single company, "Allcargo," unless a question specifically concerns which legal entity issued a
particular shipping document.
It is worth noting how each acquisition extended the network into a specific region rather than simply adding scale in
the abstract. Econocaribe Consolidators, acquired in 2013, strengthened Allcargo's presence in the Americas and the
Caribbean specifically, an origin and destination region the company had comparatively less direct presence in before
that acquisition. The stake in FCL Marine Rotterdam, acquired the same year, gave the company a stronger foothold
in one of Europe's largest container ports. Nordicon AB, acquired in 2021, extended the network into the Nordic
countries. A customer asking whether Allcargo has direct presence in a specific region can generally be answered by
tracing which of these acquisitions covers that part of the world, rather than relying only on the general 180-country
figure.

8. Sustainability & CSR
Allcargo has been building out ESG-linked initiatives across its operations, including deploying electric vehicles in its
first- and last-mile delivery fleet — the company describes itself as the first Indian logistics company to onboard more
than 100 electric vehicles for this purpose. It has also set up a dedicated Transformation Office focused on applying
technology to long-standing logistics challenges.

The electric-vehicle deployment is concentrated specifically in first- and last-mile delivery rather than long-haul
transport, which reflects where electric vehicles are currently most practical within a logistics network: short, frequent
urban and semi-urban delivery routes suit current EV range and charging infrastructure far better than long-distance
highway haulage between cities or ports. This is a useful distinction to keep in mind if a customer asks specifically
whether their long-haul shipment is being moved by an electric vehicle, since the current fleet deployment is focused
on the delivery leg closest to the end recipient.
